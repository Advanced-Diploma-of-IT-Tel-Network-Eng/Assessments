# Activity Class - Cyber security Awareness Plan

Activity for this week - “You are working as a Cyber Security Officer in an organisation. Management has asked you to prepare a detailed cyber security awareness plan for employees to reduce cyber risks in the workplace. Your task is to create a professional email to your manager/trainer explaining: current cyber security risks, awareness strategies, employee responsibilities, and recommendations for improving cyber security practices.”

Hi Teacher,

I approached the topic of the activity from my professional experience, so I developed the plan focused on two critical risk vectors currently affecting organisations in the financial sector: AI-generated deepfakes and Shadow AI (unauthorised AI tools in corporate environments).

**1\. CURRENT CYBERSECURITY RISKS**
_Deepfakes de Voz y Video_
Attackers are now capable of replicating the voice and likeness of C-level executives in real time to authorise fraudulent wire transfers, credential resets, or access grants. In the banking sector, this directly threatens internal authorisation workflows, help desk operations, and executive communication channels.

**Primary attack vectors include:**

*   Vishing calls impersonating senior management to staff
    
*   Synthetic video in virtual meetings to bypass visual identity verification
    
*   Fraudulent audio messages sent via internal communication platforms
    

_Shadow AI_
Tools deployed by employees without formal approval or oversight from IT or Security teams. This mirrors the Shadow IT problem currently; these tools can process proprietary data, client information, and internal workflows without any governance controls.

**Key exposure points include:**

*   Employees uploading sensitive documents to external AI platforms for summarisation or analysis
    
*   Unapproved AI agents integrated into business workflows without security review
    
*   Absence of data loss prevention (DLP) controls for AI-generated outputs
    

**2\. AWARENESS STRATEGIES**

_1\. For Deepfake Threats:_

*   Mandatory training modules covering voice/video impersonation techniques and real-world case studies from the financial sector
    
*   Simulated vishing exercises targeting help desk and IT support staff
    
*   Establishment of out-of-band verification protocols (a secondary confirmation channel) for any request involving access changes, financial transactions, or credential resets received via phone or video call
    

_2\. For Shadow AI:_

*   Awareness campaigns clarifying what constitutes an "approved AI tool" versus unauthorised usage
    
*   Clear internal policy communication explaining the data classification risks of feeding corporate or client data into external AI platforms
    
*   Regular internal bulletins highlighting real incidents where Shadow AI led to data exposure
    

**3\. EMPLOYEE RESPONSIBILITIES**

All employees are expected to:

1.  Never authorise any sensitive action (access grants, financial approvals, configuration changes) based solely on a phone call or video meeting, regardless of the apparent identity of the requester
    
2.  Report any suspicious voice or video communication that requests unusual access or urgent action, using the established internal incident reporting channel
    
3.  Refrain from using any AI tool not listed in the organisation's approved software inventory
    
4.  Avoid uploading internal documents, client data, database exports, or configuration files to external AI platforms
    
5.  Complete all mandatory cybersecurity training modules within the established deadlines
    
6.  Immediately notify the Security team upon discovering unauthorised AI usage within their team or department
    

**4\. RECOMMENDATIONS FOR IMPROVING CYBERSECURITY PRACTICES**

**1\. Short-term (0-30 days):**

1.  Implement a verbal code-word or challenge-response protocol for high-sensitivity requests received via voice or video - especially relevant for IT support teams managing critical infrastructure
    
2.  Deploy DLP rules to detect and block uploads of sensitive file types to unauthorised cloud/AI services
    
3.  Publish and distribute the approved AI tools inventory to all departments
    

**2\. Medium-term (30-90 days):**

*   Integrate Shadow AI detection capabilities into the existing endpoint monitoring stack, flagging unauthorised AI application usage across workstations
    
*   Conduct tabletop exercises simulating a deepfake-based social engineering attack targeting the help desk
    
*   Establish an AI Governance Committee with representatives from IT, Security, Legal, and Business units
    

**3\. Long-term (90+ days):**

*   Evaluate and implement biometric or behaviour-based identity verification for internal high-privilege operations
    
*   Develop a formal AI Acceptable Use Policy aligned with the organisation's existing Information Security Policy
    
*   Schedule quarterly awareness refreshes to keep pace with the rapid evolution of AI-generated threat techniques
