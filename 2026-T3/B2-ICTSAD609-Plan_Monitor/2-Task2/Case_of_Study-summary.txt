Case Study - WORLDUCATION

Worlducation is a 2016 social startup manufacturing B2B tablet computers for primary students, featuring AI software that adjusts to each child's learning progress. Their long-term strategy includes shifting to a B2C model. Their unique value proposition is a one-for-one model: for every classroom purchase, they donate and train a disadvantaged classroom globally, connecting both for collaborative learning.

By late 2019, they sold over 35,000 tablets to 550 schools across 23 countries. Headquarters are in the Sydney CBD, with development in Bulgaria, manufacturing in Hong Kong, and operations/marketing in Colombia, Egypt, Iceland, Russia, and the Philippines.

Following a major 2020 sale in Portugal, the COVID-19 pandemic brought hundreds of new leads but caused significant operations and production problems. The factory in Hong Kong closed down for 1 month due to government restrictions limiting supply, the sales team was overwhelmed with sale meetings over ZOOM, the tech-support team had to re-adapt the software to remote learning for many of the schools, and the founders had to start thinking on how education was going to change after this worldwide event.

Specific information needed for this assessment

The supply chain relies on an ERP System linking sales to the operations team, which authorises manufacturing requests to Hong Kong. Operations then arrange direct shipping. By April 2020, only 500 of 2000 scheduled tablets were shipped due to COVID-19 closures and China-Hong Kong tensions.

Financials:

2019 Actual: $35 million revenue, $7 million net profit, $4 million net cashflow.
2020 Budget: $45 million revenue, $9 million net profit, $5 million net cashflow.
2020 Projected: $55 million revenue, $5 million net profit, negative $3 million net cashflow (profit margin declined due to manufacturing disruptions and ad-hoc solutions).
The projected growth in revenue requires major working capital for inventory and debtors, creating expected cash flow pressure in late 2020.

Alternative business model estimates:

Second manufacturing facility: $5 million cost.
Outsourcing manufacturing: Lost margin of $200 per unit ($2 million/year for 10,000 units).
Cloud based solution (remote access on custom devices): $2 million cost.
Purchasing off the shelf tablets: Lost margin of $300 per unit ($3 million/year).
Repurposing used tablets: Lost margin of $100 per unit ($1 million/year).

Finance Authorisation Policy and Procedures

Purpose of the Policy
All finance transactions require pre-authorisation by the designated person.

Procedures
Prior to any transaction, Anna Armstrong must provide authorisation.

Use of Business Credit Card

Purpose of the Policy
Guidelines for issuing and using business credit cards.

Procedures
Cards require a complete Authorisation Form and are strictly for travel, authorised entertainment, and purchases under $500. Personal use is prohibited. Cash advances require Anna Armstrong's approval. Lost cards must be reported to the Business Manager immediately. Cardholders must reconcile monthly statements with receipts, get Anna Armstrong's approval, and forward to the Business Manager for payment. Cards must be returned upon request or termination.

New Supplier Policy

Purpose of the Policy
All new suppliers must be reviewed to ensure alignment with business objectives.

Procedures

Choosing a New Supplier: Suppliers must offer quality, service, competitive pricing, and efficient delivery. A Background Information table (Name, Location, Products, Owner, Years trading) is required.
Supplier Selection Review Checklist: Must assess pricing competitiveness, payment terms, return policies, warranties, rep knowledge, alternative suppliers, delivery services, credit checks, PPSR review, and trade checks.
Appointment of Supplier: Authorised by the Business Manager, who independently verifies bank details.
Supplier Payment Terms: Purchases need a purchase order. Terms must be at least 30 days and reviewed annually by the Business Manager (who authorises variations). Payments are reviewed quarterly to ensure adherence to terms.
Privacy Policy and Procedures

Scope
Outlines the protection of personal information under the APPs in the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth).

Collection of information: Collected from creators, publishers, IT professionals directly or via third parties (rightsholders, foreign organisations, public domain, purchased lists). Google Analytics and session cookies are used on the website. Email addresses are strictly used for their intended purpose.
Anonymity: Allowed unless impracticable or legally required to identify.
Use and disclosure of personal information: Used for primary and consented purposes.
Marketing material: Sent only if reasonably expected or consented to.
Personal information storage and security: Protected electronically and physically (servers, cloud, locked cabinets). Retained only as long as legally/operationally necessary, then securely destroyed. No direct marketing disclosures.
Digital security: External memory devices need malware scans. Files should be saved to the desktop, the drive ejected before working, and then saved to relevant folders. Weekly backups stored in the Admin security cupboard.
Internet security: Use designated mailboxes; clear inboxes. Do not open suspicious emails or attachments (trust pdf, Word, PowerPoint, Excel). No personal emails on company computers (Note: original text referenced King Edward VII College's computers). New staff undergo email competence assessments.
Performance Management Policy and Procedures

Purpose
To outline expectations for planning, managing, reviewing, and improving employee performance.

Scope
Applies to all permanent employees.

Procedures
Employees undergo two reviews annually (annual and interim). Reviews clarify duties, provide feedback, identify development, and manage underperformance via a transparent process.
Managers assess employees using standard ratings (exceeds expectations, meets expectations, development encouraged, improvement required, or unsatisfactory). Dispute resolutions escalate from the Manager to the Managing Director. High performance may trigger negotiated salary increases beyond standard increments, or promotions.

Documentation Policy and Procedures

All employees must use Worlducation templates, saving files on the cloud system using the format: [Department_Type_Detail_Version]. In-built software review functionality and feedback registers are mandatory.

Software development policy

Purpose:
Standardises software development for enterprise-level mission critical web applications using industry best practices to protect sensitive data against cyber-attacks.

Scope:
Applies to all staff, consultants, and contractors modifying or developing critical applications.

Policy Statement:
The ICT department mandates a System Development Life Cycle (SDLC) covering preliminary analysis, risk mitigation, design, development, QA, implementation, and post-review. Strict Application Development Standards must be followed. Production environments must be strictly separated from development/test environments, and development/test staff cannot access production systems.
