# Case Study: Worlducation

## Overview
Worlducation is a social startup founded in 2016 that manufactures tablet computers for primary school students. Beyond hardware, the company develops software, content, and interactive activities to engage and educate students.

Worlducation aims to transform classroom learning by implementing artificial intelligence (AI) technology that tracks each child's progress and adjusts to their individual learning needs, creating an optimal learning path.

### Business Model & Social Vision
- **Current Strategy (B2B):** Worlducation sells primarily Business-to-Business (B2B) to schools, as their solutions are most effective when integrated across an entire classroom under a teacher's guidance. This approach allows the sales team to focus on larger institutional sales and minimizes individual customer support issues.
- **Future Strategy (B2C):** The long-term plan includes expansion into Business-to-Consumer (B2C) markets.
- **Social Mission (One-for-One Model):** Worlducation envisions a world without illiteracy. For every classroom that purchases their products, Worlducation donates technology and provides training to an underprivileged classroom somewhere in the world. Furthermore, they link the buyer classroom with the recipient classroom to enable ongoing collaboration and shared learning.

### Operations & Growth
- **Growth (2016–2019):** By the end of 2019, Worlducation had sold over 35,000 tablets to more than 550 schools across 23 countries, generating revenue through hardware sales and software subscriptions.
- **Global Operations:**
  - **Headquarters:** Sydney, Australia (CBD)
  - **Development Team:** Bulgaria
  - **Manufacturing Team:** Hong Kong
  - **Operations & Marketing:** Colombia, Egypt, Iceland, Russia, and Philippines
- **COVID-19 Impact (2020):** 
  - Started 2020 with a major sale to a school in Portugal.
  - The pandemic created high demand (hundreds of new leads), resulting in unprecedented growth.
  - Rapid expansion exposed operational and production bottlenecks:
    - The Hong Kong factory was shut down for 1 month due to government restrictions.
    - The sales team was overwhelmed with Zoom sales meetings.
    - The tech-support team had to adapt software for remote/home learning.

## Supply Chain & Financial Information

### Supply Chain Process
1. **Sales Generation:** Leads generated via sales representatives or online enquiries.
2. **Order Processing:** Sales recorded in an internal Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) System.
3. **Manufacturing Request:** Operations team verifies the sales order and authorizes a manufacturing request to the factory in Hong Kong.
4. **Production Scheduling:** Factory in Hong Kong schedules and manufactures the order according to specifications.
5. **Fulfillment:** Operations team arranges freight and direct shipping to the customer.

### Operational Disruption & Risks
- **Supply Shortages:** Out of 2,000 tablets scheduled for shipment by April 2020, only 500 were completed on schedule due to factory lockdowns.
- **Geopolitical Risks:** Increasing tension between mainland China and Hong Kong creates additional supply chain uncertainty.

### Financial Overview & Projections
- **2019 Financial Results:**
  - Revenue: $35 million
  - Net Profit: $7 million
  - Net Cash Flow: $4 million
- **2020 Original Budget:**
  - Revenue: $45 million
  - Net Profit: $9 million
  - Net Cash Flow: $5 million
- **2020 Revised Management Forecast:**
  - Revenue: $55 million (driven by increased surge demand)
  - Net Profit: $5 million (reduced due to supply chain disruptions and emergency operational solutions)
  - Net Cash Flow: -$3 million (negative cash flow due to heavy working capital requirements in inventory and debtors)

### Future Business Model Options & Cost Estimates
Management is evaluating options to address capacity and supply chain issues:
1. **Establish a Second Manufacturing Facility:** Estimated capital cost of $5 million.
2. **Outsource Manufacturing to Third-Party:** Lost margin of $200 per unit ($2 million/year assuming 10,000 units sold).
3. **Develop Cloud-Based Solution:** Software accessed remotely on customer-owned devices; estimated cost of $2 million.
4. **Purchase Off-the-Shelf Tablets:** Lost margin of $300 per unit ($3 million/year assuming 10,000 units sold).
5. **Repurpose Used Tablets:** Cost/margin impact of $100 per unit ($1 million total).

### Billable / Hourly Labor Rates
- **External ICT Consultants:** $150 / hour
- **External Developers:** $100 / hour
- **Internal Business Analyst:** $80 / hour
- **Senior Management (Internal Cost):** $300 / hour

## Corporate Policies & Procedures

### 1. Finance Authorisation Policy
- **Authorisation Requirement:** All financial transactions must be pre-authorized by **Anna Armstrong** prior to execution.
- **Business Credit Card Rules:**
  - Card issuance requires an approved *Credit Card Authorisation Form*.
  - Restricted to business travel, client entertainment, and small expenses/equipment under **$500**.
  - Personal use and cash advances are strictly prohibited (unless cash advance is specifically authorized by Anna Armstrong).
  - Lost or stolen cards must be reported immediately to the Business Manager for cancellation.
  - Monthly statements must be reconciled with receipts and submitted to the Business Manager after authorization.
- **New Supplier Policy:**
  - All new suppliers must be evaluated for quality, service, competitive pricing, and delivery capacity.
  - A *Supplier Selection Background Information* form and *Review Checklist* (pricing, warranties, PPSR check, credit check, trade references) must be completed.
  - **Approval:** Business Manager authorizes new supplier onboarding and verifies bank account details independently.
  - **Payment Terms:** Standard payment terms must be a minimum of **30 days**. All orders require an approved Purchase Order (PO).

### 2. Privacy Policy & Data Protection
- **Compliance Scope:** Adheres to the Australian Privacy Principles (APPs) under the *Privacy Act 1988 (Cth)*.
- **Data Collection & Usage:**
  - Personal data collected directly from creators, publishers, licensees, and IT professionals.
  - Anonymity/pseudonyms permitted where practical.
  - Data stored in secure physical and cloud environments; retained only as long as necessary.
  - Direct marketing using personal data is prohibited without express consent.
- **Digital & Workstation Security:**
  - External storage devices (e.g., USB thumb drives) must be scanned for malware before opening.
  - Files must be edited on local desktops before saving to central drives (do not run directly from external drives).
  - Weekly system data backups required; backups stored in a locked security cupboard.
- **Email & Communications Security:**
  - Inboxes must be processed and emptied into structured folder mailboxes before logging off.
  - Suspicious emails and unauthorized attachments must be deleted immediately.
  - Personal email use on company devices is prohibited.
  - Email attachments must be restricted to standard secure formats (`.pdf`, `.docx`, `.pptx`, `.xlsx`).
  - New staff must pass an email security assessment prior to being granted internal network/email access.

### 3. Performance Management Policy
- **Review Cycle:** All permanent staff undergo two reviews per year (an Annual Formal Review and a 6-month Interim Review).
- **Performance Ratings:**
  1. Exceeds expectations
  2. Meets expectations
  3. Development encouraged
  4. Improvement required
  5. Unsatisfactory performance
- **Governance:** Reviews follow procedural fairness. Disagreements with ratings can be escalated to the Managing Director for final determination. Standard annual salary increments apply, with merit bonuses reserved for top performers.

### 4. Documentation & Governance Policies
- **Documentation Policy:** Standardized templates are mandatory. Files must follow the naming convention: `[Department_Type_Detail_Version]`. Documents are stored in central cloud storage.
- **Cloud / Host / SaaS Policy:** Third-party cloud or SaaS solutions must undergo risk assessment in coordination with the ICT Department to safeguard data security. Non-compliance is escalated to the ICT General Manager or Board of Directors.
- **Software Development Policy (SDLC):** Enterprise software development must follow a formal System Development Life Cycle (SDLC) covering analysis, risk assessment, design, coding, testing, and release. Strict separation between **Production**, **Development**, and **Test** environments must be maintained.
