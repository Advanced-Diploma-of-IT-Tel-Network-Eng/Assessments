## 2. Complete the table below to identify the legislation relevant to privacy. Identify both the federal act, as well as the act for the state or territory in which you are located.

* **Privacy Act 1988:** Its purpose is to regulate how personal information is collected, used, stored, and disclosed by Australian Government agencies and businesses of a certain size. It establishes the 13 Australian Privacy Principles (APPs) that govern data handling.
* **Telecommunications (Interception and Access) Act 1979:** Its purpose is to protect the privacy of individuals by prohibiting the unauthorized interception of communications over a telecommunications system, establishing strict rules for network access and data monitoring.

## 4. Give an example of a customer service standard or procedures that can be followed to ensure copyright, ethics and privacy.

* **Copyright: Strict Software Whitelisting Verification:** Before executing custom scripts or installing diagnostic tools on terminals, IT staff must follow a procedure to verify that the software is on the approved corporate whitelist. This guarantees the company does not use unauthorised tools, avoiding third-party copyright infringements and software licensing violations.
* **Ethics: Explicit Consent for Remote Terminal Access:** Before taking remote control of an enterprise application or accessing a server terminal to fix a live issue, the support business analyst must obtain and document explicit consent from the branch manager or internal business owner. This customer service standard ensures transparency, trust, and highly ethical IT operations.
* **Privacy: Secure Handling of Network Diagnostic Logs:** After running network diagnostics to troubleshoot connectivity between servers and terminals, a standard procedure must be followed to quickly sanitise or purge log files. This ensures that no confidential transactional data is retained unnecessarily or accessed unethically by unauthorised personnel.

## 5. Identify and explain two typical ICT security systems and procedures that can be used to manage IP, ethics and privacy in an ICT environment.

### Application Whitelisting and Execution Control

In a strictly regulated banking environment, such as the one I used to work in, application whitelisting configures servers and ATMs to run only pre-approved and legally licensed corporate software and scripts. This rigorously manages intellectual property by completely preventing the installation or execution of unlicensed or pirated software within the network. It reinforces IT ethics by ensuring that technical consultants use only authorised corporate tools for network diagnostics, and it protects privacy by automatically blocking any malware or unauthorised scripts that might attempt to extract sensitive financial data from databases.

### Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) and Terminal Audit Logging

This security procedure ensures that personnel in a role similar to mine (technical consultant) have only the precise level of access necessary to perform their specific functions. Along with active audit logging, every terminal command, database query, and server login is meticulously recorded. This manages privacy by strictly restricting who can view confidential customer information within enterprise applications. It also protects intellectual property by preventing unauthorised personnel from accessing or copying proprietary source code and promotes ethical practices by creating a transparent and immutable log that holds personnel accountable for their actions during server maintenance.

## 6. Complete the table below to identify two types of legislation relevant to intellectual property.

* **Patents Act 1990:** Its purpose is to protect new inventions, innovative processes, and methods. This grants the owner exclusive rights to commercially exploit a new device, substance, or method for a standard period of 20 years.
* **Circuit Layouts Act 1989:** Its purpose is to protect the original layout designs (topographies) of integrated circuits and computer chips, preventing the unauthorized copying of the complex hardware blueprints.
